Methodology & evaluation roadmap

EARNING THE EVIDENCE

Evaluation methodology

The engine runs reproducibly. We still need to measure whether its probabilities match real outcomes. Running more draws does not fix an inaccurate forecast.

What is ready

Pregame archive tools, source fingerprints, final-only result checks and a basic held-out evaluation report are built. The September 8 audit found 305 strongly corroborated offensive identities and zero provisional or unresolved entries. Tier counts and three scoped overrides are recorded in Data readiness.

What comes next

  1. Identity foundation complete. NFLverse Players V2 is canonical; strong corroboration requires names, team, position, current game and ESPN crosswalk agreement, with documented scoped exceptions.
  2. Collect final results. Calculate DraftKings points from final stats. Keep inactive, limited, missing and active-zero games distinct.
  3. Freeze predictions before kickoff. Preserve the forecast, model, salary, availability and source versions.
  4. Fit earlier games. Tune mean, variance, workload and correlation using chronological training data.
  5. Test untouched later games. Compare against captured ESPN projections and simple baselines, with sample counts and uncertainty by position and role.
  6. Publish the evidence. Promote only supported model components, then monitor subsequent weeks.

What calibrated would mean

A P90 forecast should be exceeded about 10% of the time across a suitable test sample. We will also check average errors, interval coverage and salary-value probabilities. There is no fixed number of weeks that guarantees enough evidence.

Player accuracy does not establish lineup tail accuracy. Contest finishes and ROI need additional ownership and field models.
